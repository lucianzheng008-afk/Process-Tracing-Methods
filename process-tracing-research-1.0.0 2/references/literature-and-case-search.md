# Literature and case search

## Search objective

Search is part of causal design. Build three independent result sets: methods, theory/mechanisms, and case evidence. Do not let a broad topical search collapse them.

## Query ladder

### Methods

Combine:

- `process tracing`, `causal mechanism`, `within-case causal inference`;
- `Bayesian`, `diagnostic evidence`, `causal process observation`;
- `case selection`, `sequence`, `counterfactual`, `active citation`;
- `theory testing`, `theory building`, `explaining outcome`.

### Theory and rivals

Search the outcome and active causal attribute with:

- mechanism verbs: `persuade`, `constrain`, `signal`, `learn`, `mobilize`, `broker`, `diffuse`, `implement`;
- competing schools and negative findings;
- scope conditions and deviant cases;
- review articles and recent citation networks.

### Case evidence

Search by actor, institution, decision venue, date window, document genre, archive series, language variants, former names, and likely bureaucratic terminology. Add rival-specific predictions and disconfirming terms.

## Source ladder

Prefer:

1. user-provided originals and local full text;
2. archives, official records, transcripts, datasets, and institutional repositories;
3. publisher pages, DOI/Crossref, library catalogues, and author repositories;
4. peer-reviewed full text;
5. reliable secondary histories and syntheses;
6. news for leads and public chronology;
7. search snippets only as navigation.

Never use a snippet, metadata record, or AI summary as evidence for a detailed empirical claim.

## Metadata and access states

Record:

- title, authors/creator, year/date, venue/repository;
- DOI, archive identifier, stable URL, or local path;
- document type and language;
- access state: `metadata`, `abstract`, `partial`, `full-text`, `primary-record`;
- pages/folio/paragraph/timestamp actually inspected;
- role: `method`, `theory`, `case`, `rival`, `source-criticism`;
- claims permitted by the inspected content.

If the full text is unavailable, use the record for discovery and bibliography only.

## Case-search stopping rule

Search until:

- every mechanism step has at least one source target;
- the best rival has an evidence plan;
- high-value observations have at least one independent verification path;
- missing evidence has an access log;
- new sources are mostly dependent or cumulative;
- known conflicts and access gaps are explicitly registered.

Time limits can stop retrieval but do not convert gaps into negative findings.

## Originality and frontier check

Before claiming contribution:

1. search the exact mechanism label and close synonyms;
2. search the C–O link without the proposed label;
3. search recent reviews and citing literature for the closest classic;
4. look for the same mechanism under another theoretical vocabulary;
5. inspect negative/deviant cases;
6. distinguish a new name from a new entity/activity, scope condition, observable implication, or integration.

Classify contribution as one or more of:

- mechanism specification;
- omitted scope condition;
- evidence that revises confidence;
- mechanism heterogeneity;
- new sequence or interaction;
- case-specific minimally sufficient explanation;
- source or measurement correction.

Do not claim novelty from wording alone.

## Search report

Deliver:

- databases/repositories and dates;
- exact query families;
- inclusion/exclusion rules;
- access failures;
- deduplicated source table;
- evidence coverage by mechanism step and rival;
- highest-priority missing records;
- provenance of every detailed case claim.
