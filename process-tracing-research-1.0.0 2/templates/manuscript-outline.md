# Process-tracing manuscript outline

## 1. Puzzle and bounded outcome

Define what happened, why it is theoretically or historically puzzling, the case and time boundary, and the inferential target.

## 2. Existing explanations and gap

Present the strongest relevant explanations, their mechanisms and scope, and the precise unresolved link. Do not equate an unused label with a literature gap.

## 3. Focal mechanism and competing explanations

Define C, O, context, mechanism entities/activities, productive continuity, alternatives, and observable propositions. Include a mechanism diagram tied to registered IDs when useful.

## 4. Research design and evidence logic

Justify the process-tracing variant, case selection, time boundary, prior, source corpus, access limits, theoretical certainty/uniqueness, empirical source evaluation, dependence treatment, and updating vocabulary.

## 5. Process reconstruction

### Step M-1-S1

Write theoretical function → case condition → entity/activity → evidence/source context → transmission → rival comparison → update/limit.

### Step M-1-S2 and subsequent steps

Repeat the same analytical sequence. Do not substitute chronology for step-level inference.

## 6. Aggregation and alternatives

Aggregate observation to proposition, proposition to step, and step to mechanism. Present mixed evidence and unresolved rivals. Add counterfactual analysis only when disciplined by actual-world evidence.

## 7. Conclusion and scope

State the bounded conclusion, contribution, weakest link, remaining evidence needs, scope conditions, and a snowballing-outward case strategy if generalization is intended.
