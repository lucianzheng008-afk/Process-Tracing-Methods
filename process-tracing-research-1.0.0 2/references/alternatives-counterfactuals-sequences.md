# Alternatives, counterfactuals, and sequences

## Three kinds of alternative

Keep these distinct:

1. **Outcome explanation:** another process could contribute to O.
2. **Mechanism variant:** the same broad C reaches O through different activities.
3. **Evidence generator:** the observation could be produced without the focal activity, for example by strategic speech, copying, record selection, or hindsight.

A project requires at least one plausible outcome or mechanism alternative and evidence-generating alternatives for vulnerable observations.

## Build rivals symmetrically

Do not use a one-word rival. For every `H-*` record:

- cause and scope conditions;
- entities, activities, and sequence;
- expected fingerprints;
- unfriendly evidence;
- overlap or incompatibility with the focal explanation;
- evidence already available and evidence still needed.

Apply the principle of **competitive fairness**: specify the strongest plausible version of the rival, using its own literature and expected process. A weak caricature cannot increase confidence in the focal mechanism.

## Adjudication matrix

For each high-value E ask:

| Question | Focal H1 | Rival H2 |
|---|---|---|
| Would E be expected? Why? | level + reason | level + reason |
| Could the source process generate E anyway? | reason | reason |
| What absence would hurt the explanation? | fingerprint | fingerprint |
| Are H1 and H2 mutually exclusive at this step? | yes/no | yes/no |

If E is expected under both, it may establish chronology or context without adjudicating. If H1 and H2 are complementary, estimate which outcome facets or mechanism steps each explains.

## Sequence as evidence

A sequence is diagnostic only relative to rival timing predictions. Specify:

- ordering constraints;
- minimum and maximum plausible lag;
- whether steps overlap;
- deadline or critical-juncture effects;
- path dependence, feedback, or threshold dynamics;
- which order a rival predicts.

“A occurred before B” is not causal evidence unless A's activity could reach B within the observed interval and the order is less expected under alternatives.

For path-dependent claims distinguish:

1. antecedent conditions;
2. critical juncture and choice set;
3. self-reinforcing or reactive sequence;
4. mechanisms of increasing returns, learning, coordination, or power shift;
5. outcome reproduction;
6. plausible points of reversal.

## Counterfactual protocol

Counterfactuals can clarify singular causal claims but are disciplined arguments, not invented alternative history.

For each counterfactual state:

1. the focal causal link;
2. antecedent change;
3. minimal-rewrite rule;
4. historical possibility at the time;
5. downstream pathway and timing;
6. background conditions held fixed and why;
7. observable actual-world features that inform the comparison;
8. sensitivity to alternative rewrites.

Use comparison cases as “natural interventions” only when similarity on relevant context is argued, not assumed.

## Mechanism breakdown

For theory revision:

1. reproduce the established mechanism from the typical case;
2. trace each step in the deviant case;
3. identify the first unsupported or failed productive link;
4. distinguish absence of activity from absence of observation;
5. compare contexts at and before the breakpoint;
6. propose the omitted condition;
7. derive a new fingerprint and test it;
8. reclassify cases if the revised scope changes the population.

Do not explain failure by restating the outcome (“policy failed because there was no implementation”). Identify the entity/activity or enabling condition that did not transmit the process.

## Conclusion discipline

Use:

- `adjudicated against`: high-value evidence is substantially less expected under a genuine rival;
- `weakened`: some rival predictions fail, but evidence or access is incomplete;
- `not supported`: no positive evidence after an adequate search, without claiming impossibility;
- `not adjudicated`: available E is similarly expected or inaccessible;
- `complementary`: rival explains another step or facet;
- `outside scope`: the project lacks evidence or theory to assess it.

Reserve `eliminated` for rare cases of genuine logical/empirical incompatibility supported by strong evidence.
