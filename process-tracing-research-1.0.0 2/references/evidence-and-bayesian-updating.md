# Evidence and Bayesian updating

## Two-stage evaluation

### Stage A: proposition to hypothesis

Assess theoretical:

- **certainty:** `P(E | H)` — would the mechanism activity produce E?
- **uniqueness:** inverse intuition from `P(E | not-H)` — could other processes produce E?

Do this before inspecting the observation when possible. State the best alternative, not an undefined catch-all.

### Stage B: observation to proposition

For found observations assess:

- semantic meaning in context;
- authenticity and accuracy;
- creator's access and competence;
- motive to distort;
- representativeness of the record;
- empirical uniqueness against source-generation alternatives.

For not-found observations assess empirical certainty: how likely the search would have detected the fingerprint if it existed.

## Qualitative Bayesian protocol

Default levels: `very low`, `low`, `medium`, `high`, `very high`, with written anchors. Do not convert them to arithmetic scores.

For each evidence item:

1. record prior level before using E;
2. rate E's expectedness under H;
3. rate E's expectedness under the best alternative H2;
4. explain the likelihood contrast;
5. adjust for source reliability and meaning;
6. adjust for dependence on evidence already counted;
7. state update direction and posterior level;
8. state sensitivity: what plausible reassessment would change the result?

A useful verbal scale:

| Likelihood contrast after source audit | Update |
|---|---|
| Similar under H and H2 | none |
| Modestly more expected under H | small up |
| Substantially more expected under H | medium up |
| Very difficult under H2 and trustworthy under H | large up |

Reverse the direction when E is less expected under H. The words describe reasoning, not universal likelihood ratios.

## When numbers are justified

Use explicit probabilities only if the analyst can:

- justify priors and likelihoods from transparent elicitation or evidence;
- keep source reliability distinct from theoretical likelihood;
- model or bracket dependence;
- conduct sensitivity analysis;
- show that conclusions do not hinge on arbitrary decimal choices.

Otherwise numbers create false precision. A structured qualitative update is superior.

## Source adjustment is not double discounting

The theoretical likelihood concerns the world if H is true. Source adjustment concerns whether the observed material accurately represents that world. Keep them in separate fields. Explain any combined judgment rather than multiplying invented values.

## Aggregation rules

### Rule 1: independent evidence can accumulate

Independent items supporting the same proposition can increase confidence. Demonstrate independence; do not assume it from different publication titles.

### Rule 2: dependence reduces marginal value

If E2 derives from E1, E2 adds no new factual information. It may still help interpret reception or diffusion, but then it is evidence for a different proposition.

### Rule 3: independent corroboration can improve accuracy

When genuinely independent and differently motivated sources converge, they may increase confidence in each observation's accuracy as well as add evidence. Too-perfect agreement may indicate copying or coordinated accounts.

### Rule 4: diminishing returns

Stop when another similar item no longer changes the proposition. Quantity cannot rescue uniformly nondiagnostic evidence.

### Rule 5: inference moves one level at a time

Aggregate observations to propositions, propositions to steps, and steps to the mechanism. Do not leap from one memorable document to the outcome-level causal claim.

### Rule 6: upper claims are capped

A mechanism claim cannot be stronger than its weakest necessary step. If mechanisms are explicitly conjunctural, use a weakest-link ceiling rather than averaging.

## Mixed evidence

Do not average labels. Compare direction and diagnostic weight. A high-certainty fingerprint that is absent under excellent access can outweigh several weak confirming observations. Conversely, inaccessible records make absence nearly uninformative.

Document conflict in the evidence register and seek:

- a source closer to the event;
- a source with different incentives;
- a trace generated independently of testimony;
- a proposition on which H and H2 diverge more sharply.

## Conclusion language

- Large, robust update across every necessary step: “strongly supports operation of the bounded mechanism.”
- Mixed but net positive: “increases confidence, with step ___ remaining open.”
- One step supported: “supports activity ___, not the complete mechanism.”
- Similar likelihood under rivals: “establishes the observation but does not adjudicate explanations.”
- Missing inaccessible record: “no update; evidence unavailable.”
- High-certainty expected trace absent under high access: “meaningfully weakens the proposition.”
